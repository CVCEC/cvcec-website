The Viet Youth Readiness & Safety Initiative

Helping newcomer Vietnamese children and their families recognize, name and safely report racism and race-based bullying, while building cultural pride and belonging.

Why this project

From silence to voice

Vietnamese parents tell us the same story: their children are teased and left out because of their English, their accent, their food or how they look, and many parents do not know how to raise a concern with the school. Vietnamese newcomer children are also rarely visible in public data, which groups them into broad “East or Southeast Asian” categories.

This project brings children, parents, youth mentors and local decision-makers together so that every child knows it is not their fault, knows who to tell, and feels proud of who they are.

The project is part of CVCEC’s plans for 2026–2027 and will go ahead if funded by the Canadian Race Relations Foundation’s National Anti-Racism Fund.

Who takes part

  • 50 children, girls and boys aged 6–12, from newcomer Vietnamese families in Willowdale
  • At least 20 parents and caregivers, in Vietnamese-language sessions
  • 10 Vietnamese-Canadian youth leaders aged 16–20, who co-facilitate every session
  • About 30 neighbours and guests from other communities at the festival
  • A Project Advisory Circle of parents, a youth leader and our Academic Director

How it works

Three phases over nine months

  1. Phase 1 · Jul–Aug 2027

    Recruit and train

    Recruit 50 children and their families through our Saturday Vietnamese class at North York Central Library, nearby schools and community groups. Screen and train 20 volunteers and youth mentors in child safeguarding and anti-racism facilitation.

  2. Phase 2 · Sep 2027–Feb 2028

    Learn together

    12 bi-weekly Saturday workshops on race- and language-based bullying, cultural pride and “who to tell and how”; library reading sessions; a three-session girls’ circle on body safety and trusted adults; an outdoor team-building day; and 5 Vietnamese-language parent sessions.

  3. Phase 3 · Jan–Mar 2028

    Share and act

    An intercultural community festival, a policy roundtable with local decision-makers, and public release of the Community Impact Report.

Bilingual Family Guide

Vietnamese–English guide on recognizing and reporting racism and bullying at school, free online after the project.

Children’s activity kit

Activities designed by CVCEC’s Vietnamese teachers in PEI and Manitoba, reusable in our Saturday classes.

Community Impact Report

An anonymized report with recommendations for schools and the City, evaluated by an independent evaluator.

Built on what already works

Rooted in our Saturday class and Viet Youth

From December 2026, CVCEC runs a weekly Saturday Vietnamese class at North York Central Library (Toronto Public Library) in Willowdale. It is the project’s home and the cultural-pride foundation for every workshop. Youth mentors come from the Viet Youth Readiness Hub, and the design draws on our Leading Urban Change project at the University of Toronto.

All adults working with children are screened, every session follows our Child Safeguarding Policy, and no names or identifying details appear in any report.
